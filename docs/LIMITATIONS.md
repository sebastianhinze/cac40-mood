# Limitations (running list for the write-up)

Each item says what the limitation is, which way it could bias results, and what we do about it.

| # | Limitation | Likely effect | Handling |
|---|-----------|---------------|----------|
| 1 | **Survivorship / selection:** the universe is today's CAC 40 (snapshot 2026-11-11). Six firms joined during or after the sample start (Thales 2019, Eurofins 2021, Accor 2024, Bureau Veritas 2024, Euronext 2025, Eiffage 2025) | Firms that did well are over-represented; average returns are flattering. Less clear for volatility forecasts | Reported, not corrected (DECISIONS #2) |
| 2 | **Three foreign filers excluded:** Airbus, ArcelorMittal, Stellantis file with the Dutch / Luxembourg regulator | Three large industrials missing | Reported (DECISIONS #3) |
| 3 | **URW excluded from the main analysis (36 firms):** Yahoo has its prices only from 2023-04-14 | One real-estate firm missing; the only listed property company in the index | Filings kept in the tables; no returns or volatility used (DECISIONS #29) |
| 4 | **Feed gap 5 Mar – 15 Jun 2020:** the official feed has about 5% of its usual volume | The COVID crash, the period with the most extreme volatility, cannot inform the announcement results | Window excluded and treated as missing, never as "no news"; particle filter predict-only; robustness run from 2020-07-01 (DECISIONS #28) |
| 5 | **Late filings:** in about 1% of documents the regulator's timestamp is more than a day after the market release; the "latest timestamp" rule then dates the event late | No look-ahead, but the day-0 reaction is missed for those events (weakens RQ1) | `ts_spread_minutes` allows dropping them as a robustness check |
| 6 | **Text coverage 96.9%:** 152 documents are not PDFs (mostly annual reports filed as ESEF packages from 2021 on), 47 are scanned images, 19 are mostly tables | The unread documents are mainly long periodic reports, not press releases | Flagged in `texts.parquet`; not scored |
| 7 | **Only the first 15 pages / about 12,000 tokens are read** (16% of documents are cut) | Tone deep inside long reports is ignored | By design (DECISIONS #20, #38) |
| 8 | **A few firms file far more than the rest:** TotalEnergies is about 18% of documents to score; its press-release filings drop sharply in 2025 | Pooled results lean on heavy filers; filing behaviour is not constant over time | Firm fixed effects; per-firm robustness in later phases |
| 9 | **Prices from Yahoo (unofficial)**, daily only | Possible revisions; no intraday reaction for the 22% of filings made during trading hours | Snapshot date recorded; intraday filings flagged, robustness shifts them to the next session |
| 10 | **LLM training-data leakage:** the model may know what happened after a release | Scores could contain hindsight | Discussed in the write-up (spec section 4) |
| 11 | **Firm size in the prompt is approximate and sometimes stale:** revenue per fiscal year is extracted by the model from results releases; TotalEnergies keeps its 2018 figure throughout. For 154 train-period documents (2019–2021) the figure was read from a filing published after the document: 35 use a later fiscal year than the 1 July rule asks for, the rest the right year's figure taken from a later release (where it may have been restated) | Materiality judgements rest on a rough size; for the 154 documents the score had an input from the future | **The 154 documents are left out of every analysis** (DECISIONS #52); the rest is flagged `size_backfilled` (DECISIONS #41, #47) |
| 12 | **No temperature control:** the scoring model cannot be made deterministic; the same document scored twice differs by about 0.02 in tone | Adds a little noise to every score | Measured in the pilot; all scores come from one cached run |
| 13 | **Lexicon baseline is my own word list** | A different list would give a different baseline | Lists are in the code in full; a translated Loughran-McDonald list is the alternative (DECISIONS #49) |
| 14 | **Local-model baseline reads single sentences** and only the first 100 prose sentences. Measured on 400 documents: no scored sentence is longer than the 128-token cut (so the models' 512-token limit never binds), but a third of the documents have more than 100 prose sentences and about 60% of all prose sentences are read | It cannot weigh a headline against a footnote, and the back part of long documents is ignored | By design of the available models (DECISIONS #50) |
| 15 | **Market/firm mood is filtered in two stages**, not jointly | The market mood is less sharp than a joint filter would make it | Validated on simulated data; stated uncertainty is well calibrated there (DECISIONS #44) |
| 16 | **Range-based variance excludes the overnight move**; GARCH forecasts are rescaled to that level | Volatility that arrives overnight (after-close announcements!) is only seen through the next session's range | Same target for every model compared; worth a robustness check with close-to-close variance |
| 17 | **Gold set: the LLM's tone scale is far more conservative than mine.** On 40 blind-labelled documents the rank correlation is 0.43 and the direction matches on 48%; I called 24 documents positive, the LLM called 15 of those neutral (it follows the prompt's materiality rule). Opposite signs are rare (2 of 40) | The scores measure "news that changes the picture for the group", not "good or bad news" as a reader would label it; small positive news is scored as nothing | Reported as it came out; scores unchanged (`reports/02_gold_agreement.md`) |
| 18 | **Gold set: low topic agreement** (35%, kappa 0.26). Largest pattern: revenue-only releases that I labelled `results` and the prompt defines as `sales_update` | Topic is a control in the event study, not a main variable; results by topic are less reliable than the label names suggest | Reported; not used as a primary feature |
| 19 | **Gold set: definitions differ between labeller and prompt, one labeller, 40 documents.** I labelled without the prompt's materiality rule and topic definitions in front of me; there is no second labeller to tell "model wrong" from "definitions differ" | Agreement numbers are a lower bound on how well the model follows its own instructions, and wide (a correlation of 0.43 on 40 documents has a 95% interval of about 0.14 to 0.66) | Stated with the result; a second pass with the definitions open would separate the two |
| 20 | **Event study, pre-event window:** with controls, surprise is related to the return on days −5…−1 (placebo). Likely causes: filings that reach the regulator's feed after the market release (#5), and hindsight in the LLM (#10) | Part of the day-0 link between surprise and returns may not be a reaction to the filing as dated | Reported next to the main result (`reports/03_event_study.md`) |
| 21 | **Mood parameters use normal reading errors**; the Student-t variant is validated on simulated data only | An extreme score moves the mood more than a robust filter would allow | Tone is bounded in [−1, 1], which limits outliers (DECISIONS #54) |
